# Changes

What each release of the tool changes, in plain words, newest first. An update's card quotes the section of the release it offers.

## 11.0.0a14

The first release made for people other than its maker, prepared on 2026-11-16 on his request for a copy that anyone can download and install, and published at `https://github.com/Sandbox-cmd/SandboxOS`. Until now the tool was built for one person on one Mac, and the sections that told its building were written to him, so they stay with him. What changed is whose name it carries, where its licence can be reached, which key seals it, and everything in it that spoke to him or about his work. How it keeps your record did not change.

- The owner's name. The licence and the first two lines of every file said "Hameed, under the Sandbox name". They now say "Hameed, under the Winktank name", by the maker's pick on 2026-11-16. Winktank is the lab that makes the tool, and the tool is still Sandbox OS. The licence is now called the Winktank Person Licence 1.0 (`LicenseRef-Winktank-Person-1.0`). Its terms are word for word what they were, but for the owner's name and the two addresses below. A lawyer has still not read it.
- The two addresses the licence had left blank. The terms live at `https://github.com/Sandbox-cmd/SandboxOS/blob/main/LICENSE`, and an organisation that wants terms of its own writes to `hi@winktank.org`.
- The key. A release is sealed (signed, so a copy can be checked against its maker) with a key, and every installed copy accepts an update only when it is sealed with the key that copy already holds. From this release the seal is made with the maker's own key, which he made himself and protects with a password; the test key of the earlier releases is retired. So a copy installed from an earlier release does not accept this release as an update. It is installed again by hand, with `uv tool install --force` and the line on the first page, and your workshop stays as it is.
- The charter and the book (the two pages placed in every workshop: what the tool promises, and how the partner talks) are a public edition. They speak to whoever installed the tool, their examples are a household budget, a balcony almanac and a collection of quotes, and the history of the maker's own rulings is off them. No promise changed its meaning. The charter names Winktank once, as who stands behind the promises, and its eighth promise says what a move is. All twenty-two lines of the book are kept lines, and none waits. Where you edited either page yourself, an update holds yours, as before.
- The table of words (the file every sentence the tool prints comes from) no longer carries the maker's own words: the names of his services and verbs and the words for his kinds of thing, 36 rows. A workshop that wants words of its own keeps them in a file of its own, `strings.local.tsv`, which the pages read. A service or a verb with no row is said by its recorded name. The table also ships without its notes column's contents, the build's own log of how each sentence came to be; a note now says only where a row falls back to English.
- The partner's standing instructions no longer call you "he": they name the maker each time. One recorded word still does, "he said", the basis a belief is written under when it rests on your words; changing it would change what records already hold, so it waits.
- The welcome no longer says the partner can work while you sleep. Nothing you can type or press starts that yet, which is what the charter says.
- The page about you said that two older copies of text about you sit in an older version of the tool. It now says so only in a workshop that came in from one.
- The first page says where the release is, what it needs, and gives the two lines that install it from there.
- The move (the command that carried the maker's older workshop into this tool) found two folders by the full path they had on his Mac. It now looks for them under the home folder of whoever runs it: `~/Sandbox` and `~/Sandbox-seventh[archived]`.
- What a release carries is now searched by a test, `tests/command/test_public_copy.py`. It holds what ships to this: no name of a path under a home folder, no address to write to but the licensor's, not the owner's old name, none of the maker's typed sentences, none of the things of his own record by name, and no sentence the tool says in English that calls you he. The suite runs at the pack in a folder whose path names no one, and the release carries the counts, not what it printed.
- The contract (`CONTRACT.md`, what each part of the tool promises the others) no longer ships: it was written to the maker and stays with him. `BUDGETS.md`, which gives each part its size limit, now gives each limit a short reason; the longer reasons, a log of how the tool was built, stay with the maker too.
- The floor (the short list of programs the tool always asks you about before they run, whatever else is set) is shorter by two programs that only its maker's own setup called for. A workshop made from now on gets no ask rule for them; a workshop that already has those rules keeps them. `stripe` is still on the list.
- `sandbox --help` no longer lists `move`, which works only with the maker's own files; typed by hand it works as before.
- What is not in the public repository: the tests, the contract and the notes of the build. They still hold the maker's own paths and his reading of his older work, so they stay with him for now. The release carries the whole suite's counts from when it was packed, in `suite-summary.json`; what the suite printed, `suite-output.txt`, stays with the maker.
- No format change: format 2 stands. No part's limit moved.

Earlier releases were built for the tool's maker on his own machine and were never published; their changes are kept with him.
