# Prophecy: barefoot shepherds competing in tall buildings

Chart step: 4 (Muhammad was a true prophet), fulfilled prophecy.
Raw source: `../../sources/provingislam/bedouins-tall-buildings.md`

## The text

"...when you see the barefoot, naked, destitute shepherds competing in constructing tall buildings."

- Sahih Muslim 8 (the hadith of Jibril, narrated by Umar ibn al-Khattab). Wording above follows this version.
- Sahih al-Bukhari 50 and Muslim 9 (narrated by Abu Hurayrah) have a shorter form ("when the shepherds of black camels compete in tall buildings").
- The "they are from the Arabs" clarification appears in a narration in Musnad Ahmad. Verify the exact reference and grading before citing it.

Both main collections date to the 9th century CE, about 1,100 years before the Gulf oil boom.

## The argument

1. The hadith was recorded centuries before the event.
2. It names a specific, unlikely group: poor Arab desert herders.
3. It predicts a specific, unlikely act: competing in tall buildings.
4. Oil was found in the Gulf in the 1930s to 1950s. Within one generation, the same people (Sheikh Zayed was born in 1918 in a pre-oil Abu Dhabi) led states building record towers.
5. The Burj Khalifa (828 m, opened 2010) is the world's tallest building. The Jeddah Tower in Saudi Arabia was designed to beat it. Construction stopped in 2018 and resumed after a 2023 restart announcement (confirm the current status).
6. A forger in 7th-century Arabia had no reason to make such a prediction. Rome or Persia would have been the obvious guess.

## Objections to prepare for

- Vagueness. "Shepherds" and "tall buildings" could fit many events, so the match may be read in after the fact. Reply: the hadith adds "barefoot, naked, destitute" and "competing", and the Arab clarification narrows it further.
- Interpretation. Some classical scholars read it as a general image of low-status people gaining wealth and status, with no specific nation meant. Reply: the literal reading now fits closely, and the two readings can coexist.
- "Mostly Bedouin" is too strong. Coastal Gulf towns before oil also held pearl divers, fishermen and traders. Tighten the claim: much of the population was poor and pre-industrial, and Bedouin tribes were a large part of it.
- "The same people, not their descendants" is hard to prove. The towers were mostly commissioned by the generation after the founders. Rely on founders such as Sheikh Zayed and on the "Rags to Riches" memoir for continuity.
- The 2024 claim needs a date check. Confirm the Burj Khalifa is still the tallest and get the current Jeddah Tower status before publishing.
- Sign of the Hour framing. The hadith presents this as a sign of the end times. Some readers take it as a warning about vanity. It does not praise the event, so present it as a prediction and avoid framing it as an achievement.
