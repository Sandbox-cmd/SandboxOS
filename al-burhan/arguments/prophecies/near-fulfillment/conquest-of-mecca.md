# Prophecy: entering the Sacred Mosque in security (Quran 48:27)

Chart step: 4 (Muhammad was a true prophet), fulfilled prophecy.
Category: arguments/prophecies/near-fulfillment
Strength: weak

## The claim

Quran 48:27, revealed just after the Muslims had been turned away from Mecca at Hudaybiyyah (628 CE), affirmed that they would certainly enter the Sacred Mosque in safety, with heads shaved or hair shortened, fearing no one. This was fulfilled by the pilgrimage of 629 and the conquest of Mecca in 630.

## Primary texts

- Quran 48:27: "Certainly Allah has fulfilled His Messenger's vision in truth: you will surely enter al-Masjid al-Haram, if Allah wills, in security, heads shaved and hair shortened, not fearing. He knew what you did not know, and granted besides that a near victory."
- Context: Surah al-Fath was revealed on the return journey from Hudaybiyyah (Dhul-Qa'dah 6 AH / March 628).
  - [Bukhari 4833](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:4833), from Umar via Aslam: "Tonight a Sura has been revealed to me, dearer to me than that on which the sun rises", then 48:1. This text says only "during one of his journeys". The identical report under the chapter "Ghazwa of al-Hudaibiya" is [Bukhari 4177](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:4177); cite that for the explicit placement.
  - [Bukhari 4834](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:4834), from Anas: "'Verily We have given you a manifest victory' refers to Al-Hudaibiya." Parallel: [4172](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:4172).
- The long treaty narrative is a single hadith, [Bukhari 2731-2732](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:2731) (Book of Conditions, from al-Miswar ibn Makhrama and Marwan). It contains Umar's protest ("Didn't you tell us that we would go to the Ka'ba and perform Tawaf?" — "Yes, but did I tell you that we would visit the Ka'ba this year?") and the Umm Salamah episode: the companions would not rise to sacrifice and shave after three commands until she advised him to go out, say nothing, sacrifice and call his barber.
- Fulfillment: the Umrat al-Qada (lesser pilgrimage) of Dhul-Qa'dah 7 AH / 629 CE under the treaty's terms, and the conquest of Mecca in Ramadan 8 AH / January 630 CE.

## The argument

1. In 628 some 1,400 unarmed-for-war pilgrims were blocked by Quraysh and accepted treaty terms widely felt among the Muslims to be humiliating, including going home without the pilgrimage. Morale was low enough that Umar openly protested and companions initially hesitated to carry out the Prophet's order to shave and sacrifice on the spot.
2. At exactly that low point, the surah called the episode a "manifest victory" (48:1) and committed to a safe entry into the Sacred Mosque, described in concrete ritual detail.
3. Within one year the Muslims performed the pilgrimage in security under the treaty; within two, Mecca itself fell almost bloodlessly and the Muslims controlled the mosque permanently.
4. The prediction was public, short-horizon, and specific, and it was made when the immediate evidence pointed the other way.

## Objections and replies

- **Self-fulfilling prophecy.** This is the decisive objection. The person announcing the entry was the commander with the means and the declared intention to bring it about. A capable leader predicting his own next objective, then achieving it, is leadership, not prescience — Alexander or Caesar could match it. Worse for the argument, the mechanism of fulfillment was partly written into the very treaty just signed: Quraysh had agreed that the Muslims would return for a three-day pilgrimage the following year. So the "prophecy" of the 629 entry was, in part, a restatement of a contract term. Reply: the stronger reading points past the treaty visit to the unconditioned, fearless, permanent entry of 630, which no treaty guaranteed and which required Quraysh — then still the stronger party in Meccan eyes — to collapse within two years. That collapse was not obviously foreseeable in 628. But the reply only mitigates: a two-year horizon for a movement already growing this fast is well within ordinary strategic confidence. Concede that this text cannot carry weight alone.
- **The verse hedges with "if Allah wills."** A prediction with an escape clause is not falsifiable. Reply: in Quranic idiom "in sha Allah" is pious etiquette commanded elsewhere (18:23-24) rather than a hedge, and the sentence structure ("la-tadkhulunna" — the emphatic "you shall surely enter") is as strong as Arabic allows. Classical commentators discussed why the particle appears here at all (various answers, e.g., it refers to which individuals would survive to enter). The reply is fair on the Arabic, but a skeptic will still note that, had the entry failed, the clause was available.
- **Dating of the verse.** A skeptic can suggest the verse was composed or placed after 630 and retrojected to Hudaybiyyah. Reply: the occasion-of-revelation reports for al-Fath are early, multiple and consistent, and the surah's whole rhetorical situation (defending a disappointing treaty, consoling a disheartened community, answering Umar-type objections) makes sense only before the conquest; a post-630 author has no need to argue that Hudaybiyyah was a victory. This internal-coherence reply is one of the better ones available, though it rests on the sira framework.
- **"Vision" framing.** The verse says God confirmed the Prophet's dream — the dream itself (of entering the mosque) is what prompted the aborted 628 pilgrimage. A skeptic reads the sequence as: dream, failed attempt, face-saving verse, second attempt succeeds. Reply: that reading is possible, but it concedes the key datum — the commitment to success was republished at the moment of failure, not after success.
