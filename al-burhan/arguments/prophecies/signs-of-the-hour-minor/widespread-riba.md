# Prophecy: everyone touched by riba

Chart step: 4 (Muhammad was a true prophet), fulfilled prophecy.
Category: arguments/prophecies/signs-of-the-hour-minor
Strength: weak

## The claim

The Prophet is reported to have said that a time will come when everyone consumes riba (usury/interest), and even the one who avoids it will be touched by its "dust" (ghubar). The modern interest-based financial system, in which virtually no one is fully insulated from interest, is offered as the fulfilment.

## Primary texts

- Abu Hurayrah: "A time will certainly come upon the people when no one will remain who does not consume riba, and whoever does not consume it will be afflicted by its dust." Commonly cited as Sunan Abi Dawud (often given as 3331) and Sunan Ibn Majah (often given as 2278); also attributed to al-Nasa'i. Numbers unverified here.
- Isnad problem, central to this file: the common chain runs through al-Hasan al-Basri from Abu Hurayrah, and many critics hold that al-Hasan did not hear from Abu Hurayrah, making the chain munqati' (broken). Al-Albani is widely reported to have graded the hadith da'if (weak) for this reason; others accept al-Hasan's narration from Abu Hurayrah or strengthen the hadith by supporting routes. All of this must be verified before citing any grading.
- Related but distinct, and better attested: general condemnations of riba and reports that riba would become widespread or be made lawful by reinterpretation (e.g. narrations about people "permitting riba by calling it trade" — attribution and authenticity also to be checked). Do not blur these into the "dust" hadith.

## The argument

1. If authentic, the hadith predicts not merely that riba will exist (it already did) but that it will become so pervasive that avoidance is impossible — even abstainers are coated by its dust.
2. Premodern economies, including usurious ones, allowed a person to live entirely outside credit markets. That changed with modern banking: fiat money, central-bank interest rates, inflation mechanics, pensions, insurance, and supply chains financed by interest mean every participant in the economy is indirectly exposed.
3. The "dust" image — indirect, unavoidable contamination rather than direct consumption — fits this structural pervasiveness unusually well.
4. Therefore the hadith anticipated a specific structural feature of the modern economy.

## Objections and replies

- Weak isnad. If the chain is broken (al-Hasan from Abu Hurayrah), the text cannot be confidently attributed to the Prophet at all, and the argument ends there. Reply: some scholars accept the chain or cite corroborating routes; but until we verify that a sound supporting route exists, honesty requires treating the hadith as weak. A prophecy argument from a weak hadith is close to worthless, whatever the fit. This objection currently stands.
- Vagueness. "A time will come when everyone consumes riba" is an open-ended moral-decline prediction of the kind every tradition produces (compare generic "sin will abound" prophecies). It names no mechanism, date, or distinguishing detail. Reply: the "dust" clause adds the distinctive note of unavoidable indirect exposure. That is a genuinely nice fit, but a short metaphor is thin evidence; a skeptic can read "dust" as ordinary rhetorical emphasis ("everyone will be tainted") with no structural meaning intended. The reply is partial at best.
- Riba was already ubiquitous. Usury was endemic in late-antique economies, including Mecca's (the Quran itself fights entrenched riba). Predicting its spread is extrapolation, not foresight. Reply: the hadith predicts totality, not spread; but see the vagueness objection — totality may be hyperbole.
- Retrofitting. "Riba" in fiqh does not map cleanly onto modern "interest"; scholars dispute which modern instruments are riba. The fulfilment claim quietly assumes the strictest definition to maximize pervasiveness. No strong reply; the file should not pretend the definitional question is settled.

Overall: the textual fit is rhetorically attractive but the hadith's grading is contested and the wording is vague. Keep the grade at weak unless verification turns up a sound corroborating chain, in which case reassess to moderate at most.
