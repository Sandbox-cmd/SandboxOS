# The expanding heaven (51:47)

Chart step: 5 (signs of a superhuman source), scientific statement.
Category: arguments/science/cosmology
Strength: weak (the least weak in this folder, but it fails criterion (a)).

## The claim

Quran 51:47 states that God is expanding the heaven, anticipating the
expansion of the universe discovered in the 20th century.

## Primary texts

Quran 51:47: "wa-l-sama'a banaynaha bi-aydin wa-inna la-musi'un" — "And the
heaven We built with strength/hands (aydin), and indeed We are musi'un."

The whole argument sits on the last word. Musi'un is the plural active
participle of awsa'a (form IV of w-s-'), whose root sense is wideness,
capacity, ampleness. Depending on construal it can mean "makers of vastness /
We made it vast", "possessors of ample power or means", or — the apologetic
reading — "We are [continuously] expanding [it]", leaning on the participle's
capacity to express ongoing action.

## The argument

1. 51:47 attributes to God, with respect to the heaven He built, the quality
   of musi'un.
2. Read as an ongoing active participle, this says God is expanding the heaven.
3. The expansion of the universe was established by Hubble's observations
   (1929, building on Slipher and Lemaitre) and is settled science.
4. No 7th-century tradition taught an expanding cosmos; Aristotelian and
   Ptolemaic cosmology, dominant in the region's learned traditions, held the
   heavens to be fixed, finished spheres. Criterion (b) arguably passes — this
   is the argument's one genuine strength.
5. Therefore the verse states something true, unknowable, and contrary to the
   science of its day.

## Objections and replies

- **Classical tafsir did not read expansion (criterion (a) fails).** The
  classical commentators read musi'un as: possessing vast power or ability
  (al-Tabari reports "dhu sa'a" — possessor of capacity); enriching/providing
  amply for creation (from wus' as provision); or having made the heaven vast.
  None describes an ongoing physical expansion of the cosmos. Reply attempt:
  the participle grammatically allows a continuous sense, and classical readers
  lacked the concept, not the grammar. But this is precisely the post-hoc
  problem: a reading no native speaker saw for 1,300 years, surfacing only
  after 1929, is being read in, not read off. The honest conclusion is that the
  verse is *compatible* with expansion, not that it *asserts* it.

- **Elastic translation.** Pre-20th-century translations render the clause as
  "We are powerful" or "We made it of wide extent" (check Sale 1734, and early
  20th-c. translations before and after Hubble; Pickthall 1930 has "We it is
  Who make the vast extent"). Modern translations saying "We are its expander"
  (e.g. Saheeh International) postdate the discovery. The translation shift
  itself is the retrofit in action.

- **Context is doxological, not cosmological.** The surrounding verses
  (51:47-49) list heaven, earth spread out (farashnaha), and pairs of created
  things, each as a sign of God's power. "Vastness/power" fits the rhetorical
  series; a technical cosmological datum would be anomalous in it.

- **Even granted the reading, "heaven" is not "spacetime".** Al-sama' in the
  Quran is the built canopy over the earth (it is "built", has lamps, can be
  rolled up). Mapping it onto the general-relativistic metric is a category
  stretch.

- **What survives.** The strongest honest form: the verse's wording does not
  *conflict* with expansion and the root meaning (wideness) is at least
  suggestive; no accessible tradition taught expansion. That is an argument
  from compatibility, not from prediction. Compatibility is worth something
  defensively (the Quran avoided asserting a static cosmos where it could
  have) but it cannot carry chart step 5 on its own.
