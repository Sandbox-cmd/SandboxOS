# Heavens and earth once joined (21:30)

Chart step: 5 (signs of a superhuman source), scientific statement.
Category: arguments/science/cosmology
Strength: weak.

## The claim

Quran 21:30 states that the heavens and the earth were a joined entity that
God split apart, and that every living thing was made from water — read by
apologists as the big bang and the aqueous basis of life.

## Primary texts

Quran 21:30: "Have those who disbelieve not seen that the heavens and the
earth were ratqan (a joined/sewn-up mass) and We fataqna-huma (split them
apart), and We made from water every living thing? Will they not believe?"

Ratq: closed, sewn together, fused. Fatq: to unstitch, split, rend. Related
verses: 41:11 (heaven was "smoke" (dukhan) and heaven and earth came "willingly
or unwillingly"), 79:30 (earth spread out after).

## The argument

1. The verse asserts an initial joined state of heavens and earth, then a
   separation.
2. Big bang cosmology describes the observable universe emerging from an
   initial hot dense state.
3. The verse asserts life's dependence on water; modern biology confirms water
   is essential to all known life.
4. The verse is addressed to disbelievers as evidence ("have they not seen").

## Objections and replies

- **Separation cosmogonies were commonplace (criterion (b) fails badly).**
  The splitting of a primeval unity of sky and earth is one of the most common
  motifs in ancient Near Eastern and world cosmogony: Egyptian separation of
  Geb (earth) and Nut (sky) by Shu; the Sumerian tradition of An and Ki
  separated (opening of Gilgamesh, Enuma Elish's splitting of Tiamat into sky
  and sea's waters); Hesiod's Gaia/Ouranos; Genesis 1's dividing of waters
  above and below the firmament, and rabbinic midrash on heaven and earth
  created together then parted. Arabia bordered, traded with, and absorbed
  lore from these cultures. On this argument's own form, a motif this common
  cannot evidence superhuman knowledge. Reply attempt: the Quran strips the
  motif of theomachy and sexual mythology. True, and theologically
  interesting, but demythologization is also present in Genesis and is not a
  scientific datum.

- **The big bang mapping is loose.** In the big bang there is no "earth" to be
  joined to "the heavens": Earth forms ~9 billion years later from supernova
  debris. The verse pictures two already-identifiable things stuck together
  and pulled apart — much closer to the Near Eastern sky/earth separation than
  to a singularity. Classical tafsir offers readings that fit the ancient
  picture, not the modern one: the heavens and earth were joined and God split
  them; or the sky was closed (no rain) and the earth closed (no growth) and
  God "split" them with rain and plants — al-Tabari records both, and the
  rain/vegetation reading suits the verse's continuation about water and life.
  So criterion (a) is at best ambiguous and the reading that best matches
  context is agricultural, not cosmological.

- **"Every living thing from water" was available knowledge.** Thales (6th c.
  BCE) made water the principle of all things; Greek and Syriac learning
  carried this. More simply, any desert people watching rain revive dead land
  (an image the Quran itself uses repeatedly, e.g. 41:39) could say it. It is
  also plausibly a statement about semen (ma' as "fluid" in 86:6, 25:54), as
  some classical commentators took it. True, but neither unknowable nor
  specific.

- **Falsifiability asymmetry.** Had steady-state cosmology won in the 1960s,
  this verse would not be cited; verses are matched to whichever cosmology
  prevails. That is the post-hoc rebuttal in its sharpest form, and there is
  no good reply.

- **What survives.** The verse is compatible with modern cosmogony and
  commendably free of mythological machinery. As with 51:47, that is a
  defensive point (no conflict), not positive evidence. Do not use in the
  chart except as "the Quran's cosmogony contains nothing it must retract."
