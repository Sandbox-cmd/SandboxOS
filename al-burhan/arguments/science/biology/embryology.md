# Embryology: nutfah, 'alaqah, mudghah (23:12-14, 22:5)

Chart step: 5 (signs of a superhuman source), scientific statement.
Category: arguments/science/biology
Strength: weak. The Galenic-precedent objection is close to decisive; this is
the genre's most famous argument and its clearest failure of criterion (b).

## The claim

The Quran's staged description of embryonic development — drop (nutfah),
clinging thing (alaqah), chewed-like lump (mudghah), bones clothed with flesh
— is claimed to be accurate and unknowable in 7th-century Arabia.

## Primary texts

Quran 23:12-14: "We created man from an extract of clay; then We made him a
nutfah (drop) in a firm lodging; then We made the nutfah an 'alaqah; then We
made the 'alaqah a mudghah; then We made the mudghah bones ('izam); then We
clothed the bones with flesh (lahm); then We produced him as another creation."

Quran 22:5: "...We created you from dust, then from a nutfah, then from an
'alaqah, then from a mudghah, formed and unformed (mukhallaqah wa ghayr
mukhallaqah)..." Also 75:37-39, 96:2 ("created man from 'alaq"), 86:6-7
(emitted fluid from between the sulb and tara'ib), and hadith Sahih al-Bukhari
3208 / Sahih Muslim 2643 (forty days as nutfah, then 'alaqah the like of it,
then mudghah the like of it).

Key term: 'alaqah, root '-l-q (to cling, attach). Classical senses: clot of
blood (the dominant classical gloss), leech, clinging/suspended thing.

## The apologetic case (strongest form)

1. The sequence drop → clinging stage → lump → skeleton → flesh broadly tracks
   real development: implantation ("clinging"), the somite-stage embryo (said
   to resemble a chewed lump with somites as "teeth marks"), ossification
   beginning before the main growth of muscle mass.
2. 'Alaqah as "leech-like clinging thing" matches the implanted embryo's
   appearance and its drawing of nourishment from the mother — a triple pun
   (clot/leech/clinging) each facet of which fits.
3. The Quran corrects Aristotle's view that the female contributes no seed:
   86:6-7 and the "mingled drop" (nutfah amshaj, 76:2) imply both parents'
   fluids.
4. Keith Moore (embryologist, *The Developing Human*) endorsed the
   correspondence in a special 1983 edition with Islamic additions; this is
   the argument's standard modern authority.

## Objections and replies

- **Galen's stages were available (criterion (b) fails).** Galen (On Semen, On
  the Formation of the Foetus, 2nd c. CE) describes four stages: (1) seminal
  fluid, (2) a bloody, unformed stage (his second stage is literally filled
  with blood), (3) a fleshy, still-unarticulated lump, (4) articulation of
  parts, with bones formed and then flesh around them. The parallel to
  nutfah → 'alaqah (blood) → mudghah (flesh-lump) → bones then clothed with
  flesh is very close, down to the order "bones, then flesh" — which is
  Galenic doctrine but not quite how development works (cartilage models,
  muscle, and ossification develop concurrently, not sequentially).
  Transmission routes existed: Galen was translated into Syriac (Sergius of
  Resh'ayna, d. 536) and taught in the Syriac medical milieu (Jundishapur's
  prominence is debated but Syriac medicine in Mesopotamia is not); al-Harith
  ibn Kalada, reputed "physician of the Arabs" and a contemporary of the
  Prophet, is reported (in late sources) to have studied Persian/Greek
  medicine. The ideas also circulated in Jewish tradition (Talmudic embryology
  speaks of early stages as fluid and clot-like). Reply attempt: no Arabic
  translation of Galen existed before the 9th century, the Prophet was
  unlettered, and the Quran omits Galen's errors (e.g. male/female semen
  ripening). Counter: oral circulation of medical commonplaces does not
  require books, "stages of the womb" talk is exactly the kind of lore
  midwives and physicians trade; and the Quran/hadith arguably retain Galenic
  errors (bones-then-flesh sequence; hadith Bukhari 3208's 40+40+40-day
  timetable puts the 'alaqah at 40-80 days, far too late for "clot/clinging"
  and conflicting with observable miscarriage evidence). On this argument's
  own form, a close match to the region's dominant medical school is evidence
  of a shared environment. This objection stands; the replies only soften it.

- **'Alaqah translation is elastic.** Every classical commentator and lexicon
  foregrounds "clot of (congealed) blood" — a false description of the embryo,
  which is never a clot — and classical tafsir of the stages reads them via
  blood (e.g. "then the drop becomes blood"). "Leech" and "suspended thing"
  are attested senses of the root but their application to this verse is
  modern, popularized via Moore/Zindani in the 1980s. Choosing the one
  non-falsified sense after the fact is the elastic-translation rebuttal in
  textbook form. Criterion (a) therefore fails: the natural classical reading
  says "clot", and the clot reading is wrong.

- **The Keith Moore endorsement is compromised as authority.** The embryology
  additions appeared in a special edition produced with Abdul Majeed
  al-Zindani under Saudi sponsorship (Commission on Scientific Signs); Moore
  largely declined to defend the claims in later secular editions or
  interviews. Citing it without this context would be dishonest; with the
  context, it carries little weight.

- **Sulb and tara'ib (86:6-7).** Often read as semen issuing "from between the
  backbone and ribs", which is wrong anatomically; apologetic rereadings
  (referring to the gonads' embryonic origin near the kidneys, or to the
  couple rather than the fluid) are possible but strained. A verse requiring
  rescue cannot simultaneously serve as evidence.

- **What survives.** Honestly: little, for chart purposes. The description is
  a fair poetic summary of development as late-antique medicine understood it,
  free of some pagan mythology, and serviceable for its homiletic point
  (humility about origins, resurrection). The claim of unknowable accuracy
  does not survive the Galenic parallel plus the 'alaqah problem. Do not use
  in the chart; keep this file as the worked refutation to cite when the
  argument is urged on us.
